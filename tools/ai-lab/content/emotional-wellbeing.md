## About this wellbeing information

This information is general and supportive. It is not therapy or a diagnosis. If your feelings are hard to manage, your doctor, nurse or care team can help you find the right support.

## Common feelings with a heart condition

Living with a heart condition can bring many feelings: worry about the future, fear when the body feels different, sadness, frustration about things that are harder now, or feeling less like yourself. These feelings are common and understandable. They often ease with time, support and small daily steps.

## Stress

Stress can show in the body and the mind: tense shoulders, a fast heartbeat, shallow breathing, trouble sleeping, or feeling irritable or on edge.

Things that can help:

- Pause and take a few slow breaths, letting the out-breath be a little longer than the in-breath.
- Notice the support beneath you: the chair, the floor, your feet.
- Break big tasks into small steps and do one at a time.
- Make time each day for something calming that you enjoy.

The Grounding and Follow the breath practices in the app are designed for moments like these.

## Worry and anxiety

Worry is natural, especially about health. It can become hard when it is constant or stops you from doing things.

You could try:

- Setting aside a short worry time each day, and gently putting worries aside until then.
- Grounding: name five things you can see, four you can feel, three you can hear, two you can smell and one you can taste.
- Writing worries down in the journal, then one small thing you can do about each.

If you are worried about a symptom, it is better to ask your care team than to keep wondering.

## Low mood

Everyone feels down sometimes. It can help to keep a simple daily routine, stay in touch with people, get some daylight and gentle movement, and plan one small thing to look forward to each day.

Talk to your doctor or care team if you feel low, hopeless or have lost interest in things on most days for two weeks or more. Low mood is common with heart conditions and can be helped.

## Sleep

Good sleep supports both mood and the heart. It can help to go to bed and get up at similar times, keep the bedroom quiet and dark, avoid caffeine later in the day, and do something calming before bed, such as the Follow the breath practice.

If breathlessness makes it hard to lie flat or wakes you at night, tell your care team, because this can be a sign to check.

## Feeling alone

Feeling alone or misunderstood is common after a health change. Reaching out can help: a call or visit with family or friends, a community or faith group, or a support group for people with heart conditions. Your care team may know of groups near you, including cardiac rehabilitation programmes where you can meet others.

## Practices in the EmbraceAI app

The app offers short guided practices that can help you settle:

- Grounding: noticing the support beneath you, then following your breath. Often helpful when stress or worry feels strong.
- Follow the breath: going straight to a calm breathing practice. Often helpful when you are tired or want something simple.

Each can be done as a short practice (about 3 minutes) or a full practice (about 5 to 6 minutes). Afterwards you can write what you noticed in the journal.

## When to get more support

Contact your doctor or care team if your feelings are hard to cope with, last a long time, or affect your sleep, eating or daily life.

If you have thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life, get help now: call your local emergency number or a crisis line, or tell someone you trust straight away.
