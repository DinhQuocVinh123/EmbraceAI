## About this healthy living information

This information gives general ideas for looking after your heart at home. It is not a personal plan. Your doctor, nurse, dietitian or care team may give you advice that is different for your condition, and their advice comes first.

## Eating for heart health

A heart-healthy way of eating usually includes:

- Plenty of vegetables and fruit.
- Wholegrains, such as brown rice, oats and wholemeal bread.
- Beans, lentils, nuts, fish and lean meat.
- Less processed food, sugary food and drinks, and fried or fatty food.

Small changes, kept up over time, matter more than a perfect diet. If you have diabetes, kidney problems or another condition, ask your care team or a dietitian what suits you.

## Salt

Too much salt can raise blood pressure and make the body hold on to fluid. The World Health Organization recommends less than 5 grams of salt a day for adults, about one level teaspoon, and most of it is already in food we buy.

To eat less salt, cook with herbs, spices, garlic, lemon or ginger instead of salt or salty sauces, taste food before adding salt, and choose lower-salt versions of bread, sauces, soups and snacks. Your care team may give you a different salt limit.

## Fluids

Some people with heart conditions are asked to limit how much they drink, and others are not. Follow what your care team told you, and do not change it on your own. If you are not sure, ask at your next appointment.

## Physical activity

Regular activity helps the heart, mood, sleep and energy. The World Health Organization suggests that adults, including older adults, aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, such as brisk walking, as their health allows.

With a heart condition, start gently and build up slowly. Walking, light gardening and gentle exercise are good starts. You should be able to talk while moving. Stop and rest if you feel chest pain, severe breathlessness, dizziness or an irregular heartbeat, and tell your care team. Ask your care team about a cardiac rehabilitation programme: it helps people exercise safely.

## Smoking and alcohol

Stopping smoking is one of the best things you can do for your heart, at any age. Your doctor or pharmacist can help with support and treatments to quit.

Alcohol can affect blood pressure, heart rhythm and some medicines. Ask your care team how much, if any, is safe for you.

## Checking your health at home

Your care team may ask you to check some things at home, such as blood pressure, weight or pulse. If they do, check at the same time of day, write the results down, and bring them to your appointments.

Follow the targets and limits your care team gave you, and contact them if your results are outside those limits.

## Rest and pacing

Balance activity and rest through the day. Plan the harder tasks for when you have the most energy, take short breaks, and ask for help with heavy jobs. Good sleep and regular rest help your body recover.
