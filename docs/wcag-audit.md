# Colour contrast audit — WCAG 2.2

Generated 2026-09-20 by `tools/a11y/report.py`. Every number
below is measured from the colours the application actually renders, not from a
style guide. Re-run the tool after any colour change; do not edit this file.

## Result

**58 of 58 pairs pass.** The narrowest margin
is *"Not great" bar against its track* at 3.53:1 against a 3.0:1 requirement.

The same thresholds are enforced by `test/contrast_test.dart`, which runs with
the ordinary test suite. A colour change that drops below a threshold fails the
build rather than quietly shipping.

## Method

Contrast is computed per WCAG 2.2 from relative luminance, with sRGB values
linearised before weighting. Thresholds: SC 1.4.3 requires 4.5:1 for normal
text and 3:1 for large text (>=24px, or >=18.7px bold); SC 1.4.11 requires
3:1 for user-interface components and for graphical objects that carry meaning.

Pairs were chosen because they appear on screen, not because they exist in the
palette. Semi-transparent layers are blended first, so what is measured is what
the eye receives.

## Text over video

This is the case a palette-only audit misses. Captions are positioned against
the screen, not against the video frame, so on a short, wide window — a phone
browser held sideways, a tablet — they fall on the picture itself.

All 14,400 frames of both settings were scanned for the brightest band a line
of text could land on. The worst is a relative luminance of 0.531,
which gives white text **1.81:1** — well below the 4.5:1 required.

Three fixes followed:

| Layer | Treatment | Worst case now |
|---|---|---|
| Caption | Opaque backing plate at 62% black | 6.19:1 |
| Top and bottom bars | Scrim held at 65% black across the text area before fading | 7.00:1 |
| Small labels | Changed from 70% white to full white | as above |

The plate is computed against pure white, so those figures are floors that hold
for any frame. Over the black letterbox on a phone the plate is invisible, so
nothing changed on the layout most people will see.

## What changed to get here

Five mood colours were failing in the light theme, between 1.28:1 and 2.85:1.
They were Material 300-weight tints — comfortable on a dark background,
invisible on a light one. Each mood now carries two shades and picks the one
that suits the background.

Tuning them exposed a second problem the eye would have caught eventually: the
darkened amber for "Okay" landed 22 degrees of hue from the darkened orange for
"Not great", at almost identical lightness. Two adjacent levels on a five-point
scale looked like one colour. "Okay" was moved towards olive.

The bottom-sheet drag handle used a divider colour at 1.62:1. It tells the user
the sheet can be pulled, so it carries information and now uses a visible
outline colour.

Automated coverage now checks semantic headings, selected states, live
captions, spoken progress values, large system text, reduced motion and the
Space/F1/Escape keyboard flow. A manual audit with TalkBack, VoiceOver,
hardware switch controls and people with low vision is still required before
claiming conformance for a clinical release.
